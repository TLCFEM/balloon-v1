\section{Conclusions}
In this work, we have presented a flexible constitutive model for metals based on the subloading surface concept.
Unlike other models in the literature, the proposed model does not introduce additional memory surfaces, but instead relies on a split of plastic strain, which drives the evolution of various scalar bounds.
Based on this split, a novel isotropic hardening formulation is proposed.
The proposed formulation serves as an alternative to existing kinematic--style approaches.
The formulation remains relatively simple, close to the original one \citep{Chang2025}, and is straightforward to implement in an efficient manner.

Through a series of numerical examples, the proposed model is proved to be capable of capturing complex cyclic behaviour, including stable hysteresis loops, isotropic hardening stagnation, cyclic hardening and softening and improved ratcheting response.
Further work may focus on the multiaxial behaviour of the proposed model.

The proposed \texttt{Balloon}-v1 model is implemented in \texttt{suanPan} \citep{Chang2024}.
All numerical models and the relevant scripts are available in this repository\footnote{https://github.com/TLCFEM/balloon-v1}.
