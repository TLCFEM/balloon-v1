\section{Conclusions}
In this work, we have presented a flexible constitutive model for metals based on the subloading surface concept.
The proposed model is capable of capturing complex cyclic behaviour, including stable hysteresis loops, isotropic hardening stagnation, cyclic hardening and softening and improved ratcheting response.
Unlike other models in the literature, the proposed model does not introduce additional memory surfaces, but instead relies on a split of plastic strain, which drives the evolution of various scalar bounds.
The formulation remains relatively simple, close to the original one \citep{Chang2025}, and is straightforward to implement in an efficient manner.
